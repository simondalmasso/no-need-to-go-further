# Story bible — no need to go further

## Logline

A university student moves through an aggressively ordinary morning until she sees herself standing across campus. She follows the anomaly. From then on, reality begins behaving like a dream that is slowly becoming aware of the person watching it.

There is no spoken dialogue and no explanatory text.

## Dramatic law

**Before the anomaly:** repetition, symmetry, routine, natural sound, stable lenses, stable geography.

**After the anomaly:** every scene violates exactly one rule at first. Later scenes violate several rules simultaneously.

The viewer should not ask “what monster is chasing her?” The viewer should ask “which part of reality can I still trust?”

## Borrowed concepts, transformed

### Identity instability
From the territory explored by *A Scanner Darkly*: the protagonist becomes both observer and observed. Reflections lag. Her shadow makes independent choices. Alternate versions of her occupy adjacent spaces.

### Manufactured reality
From *Dark City*: architecture can be rewritten while inhabitants behave as though nothing changed. University corridors lengthen. A classroom becomes a bus. A staircase exits into a childhood bedroom.

### Temporal wound
From *Donnie Darko*: one mundane timestamp repeats as an omen. Clocks disagree with movement. Events appear to remember having happened before.

### Nested space
From *Inception*: depth is expressed spatially, not through exposition. Going “deeper” means entering locations embedded inside locations: mirror → bedroom → window → inverted city.

### Reality/game ambiguity
From *eXistenZ*: interaction should feel physical and diegetic. The viewer never sees a game menu. Touching a door, reflection, stair, object, face or light *is* the interface.

### Perception motif
The idea that observation changes the scene is used as surreal fiction only. Looking at an anomaly may stabilize it; looking away may let it move.

# ACT I — THE BORING WORLD

## 01 — WAKE

Close, repetitive fragments.

Alarm. Sheet. Eye. Toothbrush. Water. Toast. Shoes. Elevator.

No score.

Sound: compressor hum, water pipes, distant traffic, fridge relay, fabric, keys.

Visual law: locked or extremely controlled camera. Flat morning light.

## 02 — COMMUTE

Bus.

Same gestures repeated by different passengers.

A woman scratches her eyebrow.
A student taps a shoe.
Driver checks mirror.
Traffic light.

Repeat the sequence once, almost identically.

One impossible detail appears for two frames: every passenger is looking at her.

She does not notice.

## 03 — UNIVERSITY

Fluorescent corridors. Stairs. Classroom.

She sits.

Notebook.
Pen.
Projector fan.
Chair squeak.
Rain starting against glass.

She looks outside.

## 04 — THE ANOMALY

Across the courtyard stands **another version of her**.

Everyone else is moving.
The duplicate is perfectly still.

The duplicate slowly raises one arm and points toward a service corridor.

A wall clock reaches the recurring time.

The second hand stops.

Ambient sound drains away frequency by frequency until only sub-bass remains.

The duplicate walks out of frame.

# CHOICE 01 — LEAVE / FOLLOW

The class empties.

At an intersection:
- one direction leads toward daylight / exit;
- the other toward the service corridor.

There are no labels.

The camera breathes between both possibilities.

Hotspots are attached to physical geometry, not UI boxes.

Timeout default: **FOLLOW**.

### LEAVE

Outside appears normal.

At a storefront reflection, the protagonist moves one beat before her reflection.

Then the reflection remains still while she moves.

Behind the reflection: the duplicate.

She turns around. Nobody.

On returning to the glass, the reflected street is now night.

A passing bus wipes the frame.

When it clears, she is back inside the university corridor.

### FOLLOW

The service corridor is initially plausible.

Cut.

It is longer.

Cut.

Door spacing changes.

Cut.

Lights illuminate ahead of her and die behind her.

One door opens onto the classroom she just left. Inside, she can see herself still sitting at the desk.

She approaches.

The door closes.

When she opens it again: university bathroom.

Both paths converge here.

# CHOICE 02 — MIRROR / STALL

Bathroom.

Water dripping.

One fluorescent tube cannot fully ignite.

Mirror: her reflection continues breathing after she holds her breath.

Closed stall: three soft knocks from inside.

User may touch:
- mirror;
- stall door.

Timeout default: **STALL**.

### MIRROR

Her palm approaches the glass.

The reflected palm withdraws.

The mirror acquires impossible depth.

Inside it is her bedroom from the morning, rotated ninety degrees.

She crosses.

Gravity belongs to the room, not to her.

Coffee hangs in the air.
Curtain falls sideways.
Outside the window, the city is above her instead of below.

The window becomes a passage.

### STALL

Door opens into an empty city bus.

She enters.

Bathroom disappears when the door closes.

The bus begins moving.

Outside its windows, locations pass as though they were stations:
- classroom;
- childhood kitchen;
- flooded supermarket;
- empty lecture hall at night;
- dense forest;
- the same bathroom.

The duplicate runs alongside the bus without effort.

She points upward.

The bus doors open onto a staircase.

Both paths converge.

---

# CHOICE 03 — UP / DOWN

A concrete staircase without visible end.

Up: warm daylight that pulses like breathing.

Down: cold fluorescent darkness and a distant mechanical rhythm.

Timeout default: **DOWN**.

### UP — MEMORY WEATHER

Each landing is a different version of the protagonist's life.

No exposition; only impossible visual evidence.

A child's birthday with nobody moving.
Her bedroom twenty years older.
A graduation photograph with her face absent.
A hospital corridor where every patient is her.

The higher she goes, the more unfinished the environments become.

Eventually she steps above the architecture.

The university is a small illuminated structure suspended in black space.

Then another appears.

Then hundreds.

### DOWN — ARCHIVE

Parking level.
Maintenance tunnels.
Concrete service room.
Bare earth.
Something resembling architecture grown rather than built.

At the bottom: a CRT monitor.

It shows the opening shot of the film.

The image rewinds.

Morning.
Previous night.
Childhood.
Before birth.

The recordings continue.

---

# CHOICE 04 — CLOCK / VOID

The duplicate returns.

Her appearance no longer remains stable between cuts.

In one hand: the alarm clock from the first shot.

In the other: an impossible black absence that occludes whatever passes behind it.

No dialogue.

Timeout default is deterministic per session seed so the passive viewer does not always receive the same ending.

### CLOCK — RESET

She touches it.

Alarm.

Opening morning repeats almost shot for shot.

Tiny changes:
- opposite hand turns off alarm;
- bus driver is different;
- a building is missing.

At university she looks outside.

No duplicate.

Clock finally moves past the recurring time.

She relaxes.

Cut to her empty bedroom.

Alarm clock displays the recurring time again.

Black.

### VOID — BREAK

She touches the absence.

Spatial rules fail without using a conventional “digital glitch.”

Foreground becomes background.
Objects persist across cuts where they should disappear.
Perspective vanishes.
The sky passes through the building.
Her face occupies several adjacent moments.

All mundane sounds from Act I return simultaneously and become overwhelming.

White.

She opens her eyes somewhere we have never seen.

Black.

# Interaction language

1. A scene never pauses in its emotional middle just to ask a question.
2. Choice begins on a held ending shot or a deliberately composed transition shot.
3. Interactive targets must already exist diegetically in the frame.
4. Hover/touch feedback is almost subliminal: slight parallax, focus breathing, local sound shift.
5. The timeout is communicated through the world becoming impatient, not a visible countdown.
6. A passive viewer still sees a complete film.
7. An active viewer should discover that earlier choices alter later details, not only which clip plays next.

# Sound language

No dialogue.

Three sonic strata:
- **WORLD:** traffic, fluorescent hum, plumbing, footsteps, room tone.
- **BODY:** breathing, cloth, pulse, swallowing, shoe impact.
- **ANOMALY:** reversed transients, infrasound-like drones, impossible reverb, repeated environmental samples displaced in time.

After the anomaly, familiar sounds should become structurally wrong before they become obviously supernatural.
